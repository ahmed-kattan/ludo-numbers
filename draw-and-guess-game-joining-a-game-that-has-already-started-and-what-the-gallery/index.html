Draw and guess game: joining a game that has already started, and what the gallery keeps at the end

Ahmed Moaz · 4 November 2026

Two things in a draw and guess game, which works well as a party game, happen at the edges. One is a friend who turns up after the first drawing has begun. The other is what is left on the table when the last drawing is done. Both were reworked on 18 October, so I can say what each does and where its limits are.

Can a friend join a draw and guess game that has already started?

Yes. Someone who opens the link during a game is seated at once. They see a short notice that their place is being taken, and then they can guess the drawing on screen. They do not have to wait for the next turn, and they score like anybody else from the first guess. A newcomer cannot draw yet, of course. They are added to the end of the drawing order, so their own turn comes when the lap reaches the end.

This was not always so. Until 18 October a latecomer was queued and had to watch a whole drawing before they could type a letter. Now the first drawing they see is one they can win points on.

The only thing that stops a join is a full table. If every seat is taken, the game says so.

What happens if someone leaves and comes back?

A player who left can sit down again and gets their score back. A player who was voted out by the table cannot return to that game.

There is one more way out, and it is automatic. If a player's own drawing turn is ended by the clock with nothing on the page, twice running, the game takes them out of the way so the others are not left staring at a blank canvas. They can sit down again at a later turn. Anything the player does themselves, such as picking a word or guessing right, starts that count again.

Does a late joiner cost the others anything?

Slightly, and honestly it is worth it. More players dilute the drawer's average, since the drawer's pay is shared over everyone else. Each extra right answer also cuts the share of the guesses that follow. A newcomer also adds a drawing to every lap they stay for, so the game grows by one turn per round.

So a table of six that gains a seventh player in round one goes from eighteen drawings to twenty-one. At the 100-second default, that is at most 300 more seconds of drawing across the game.

What does the gallery keep?

When the game ends, the results screen shows a strip of every drawing from the game. Each one carries its word and the name of the player who drew it. A button opens them all as a grid, with a count of how many you drew yourself. Tap one and it opens large.

From there you can save a single drawing, or every drawing as one picture, with a title and a caption under each. On a phone the share sheet offers the file to your apps, and elsewhere it downloads as an image.

The drawings appear only after the game is over. Until then the words are still being guessed, and a gallery would give the answers away. Only people who were at the table can open it. If you reload the page or arrive late at the results, the pictures are still there. The last one reaches the gallery as its reveal ends, a moment before the results, so the screen asks for the set twice.

The gallery holds up to 120 pictures. The biggest table, twelve players on two rounds, makes 24.

Try both features

Boardit's Draw and Guess is a free drawing game online with friends, and you can test a late join by opening the link on a second device while a game is running.

Two small habits

Tell latecomers they can start guessing at once. Then, at the end, give the table a minute with the gallery before anyone closes the page.
