Flag trivia questions on Europe: ten flags that a stripe, a cross or a colour gives away

Ahmed Moaz · 3 November 2026

Flags suit a quiz because you can describe one in a sentence and the listener can still picture it. These flag trivia questions are all about Europe, and each gives only colours and shapes, so you can read them down a phone line or over a video call with no picture shared. They run from easy to hard and make a tidy round for a trivia quiz or one slot in a longer trivia night, with a short story after every answer.

Ten flags, ten descriptions

  1. Which country's flag is three plain vertical stripes of green, white and red?
  2. Which country's flag has black, red and gold stripes laid out horizontally?
  3. Red, white and blue horizontal stripes, with red on top: whose flag is that?
  4. White, blue and red horizontal stripes, from top to bottom. Which country?
  5. Which country's flag is red and yellow, with the yellow band twice as wide as each red one?
  6. A yellow cross on a blue background is the flag of which country?
  7. A blue cross on a white background is the flag of which country?
  8. Which European country's flag is white above red?
  9. Which country's flag has red, white and red horizontal stripes?
  10. A white X-shaped cross on a blue background is the flag of which nation?

The answers, and a line to say after each

  1. Italy. Mexico uses the same three colours but puts an eagle in the middle.
  2. Germany. Belgium has the same colours in vertical stripes.
  3. The Netherlands. The top stripe was orange at first, after the House of Orange.
  4. Russia. Peter the Great is said to have based it on the Dutch flag, which is why the colours match and only the order changes.
  5. Spain. The national coat of arms sits on the yellow stripe.
  6. Sweden. The colours may come from the coat of arms of the Folkung dynasty.
  7. Finland. White is for snow and blue for the country's thousands of lakes.
  8. Poland. The colours come from its coat of arms, a white eagle on a red field.
  9. Austria. The design is traced to the 1200s, which makes it one of the oldest flags still flying.
  10. Scotland. The Saltire is linked to St Andrew and is said to be among the oldest national flags.

Which flags do tables mix up?

Questions 3 and 4 are a trap on purpose. The Netherlands and Russia use the same three colours, so a player who only remembers "red, white and blue" has a coin flip, and the player who remembers where the red sits wins. Put them back to back if you want to make the point about the Dutch link.

Questions 1 and 2 each have a neighbour that causes an argument. Someone will say Mexico for the first and Belgium for the second. Both are close, and both are wrong, so read the clue about the stripes slowly.

Question 8 is the one I expect to go wrong. White over red sounds easy, but many players have Indonesia or Monaco in their heads, where the colours sit the other way up.

How do you ask flag questions with nothing to look at?

Say the colours in order, top to bottom or left to right, and say which way the stripes run. Add no more hints than that. To make it harder, drop the word "European" and let the table work out where each flag belongs.

For a bigger group, hand out paper and ask for written answers before anyone speaks. Reveal them together, then read the story for each one while people compare.

A note for the scorekeeper

Decide before you start whether a spelling counts. "Holland" for the Netherlands is a common slip, and I would accept it. Answers such as "Deutschland" are fine too, since the flag is the same in any language.
