Drawing games online for a classroom: tables of ten, a word list from your unit, and the gallery as a review sheet

Ahmed Moaz · 3 November 2026

Drawing games online can work in a classroom for a plain reason, and the party game spirit helps. A student who cannot yet explain an idea in a sentence can often sketch it, and the others show they understood it by naming it. Before anything else, there is an age line. Under-13s are outside Boardit's terms, so this plan is for classes of 13 and over. For younger classes, a paper version of the same game does the job.

How many students can share one table?

Each table holds up to twelve players. A class of thirty therefore needs three tables, each with its own host. A teacher can host one and name two students to host the others. Ten per table is a comfortable size: every student draws at least once, and there are nine people to guess each picture.

Students open the page on whatever they have, a phone, a laptop or a tablet. Guesses are typed, so the room stays quiet. That helps a class that sits in rows, and the same tables work over a video call if the class is remote.

What settings suit a lesson?

For a lesson I would choose a 40-second clock and two rounds, which is the shortest game on offer. A round means every student draws once, so a table of ten draws twenty pictures. The arithmetic is simple. Twenty drawings at 40 seconds is 800 seconds. Add up to twelve seconds for each drawer to choose a word and six seconds for each reveal, and the whole game takes about 19 minutes at the most. Most turns end sooner, because a turn stops when everyone has guessed.

That leaves time to introduce the game, play it and talk about the result inside one lesson of forty minutes.

Where does the word list come from?

The host types a list of words separated by commas. Each can be up to 32 characters, so "photosynthesis" and "the water cycle" both fit. Duplicates are dropped. A word that the game's profanity filter would mask is dropped too, which stops an awkward word reaching a drawer. The list needs ten words to be played on its own, and the players never see it.

Here is the catch I would tell any teacher. Every word shown to a drawer is marked as used, not only the one they pick. If each drawer is offered two words, twenty drawings use up forty words. Offer five and the same game uses a hundred. When the list runs out, the game deals the whole list again, so a short list means repeats. Set the choice to two words and write at least as many words as the game will deal.

What can the gallery do for the review?

When the game ends, the drawings are collected. Everyone at the table can open a strip of them, each shown with its word and the name of the student who drew it, and save one picture or the whole game as a single image. Ask each table to pick the clearest drawing and the most confusing one, and explain the difference. The confusing picture is usually the one that shows a half-understood idea, which is where a teacher wants to look.

The gallery opens only after the game finishes, and only for people who sat at the table.

What if a student draws something unkind?

Drawing games invite rude doodles, and nothing replaces a teacher watching the room. The game does offer one tool: the table can vote a player out. A vote needs two fewer votes than there are players, and never fewer than two, so it takes most of a table to agree. Say the rule before the lesson starts, and the vote rarely matters.

Try a short test run

To see the settings before a lesson, a trial table with friends or colleagues on Boardit's Draw and Guess, a free drawing game, is enough, and two or three people will do.

A lesson in five steps

  1. Split the class into tables of ten at most, and name a host for each.
  2. Lock each table with the padlock in the lobby before sharing its link, because a new table opens public and shows in Open tables, where anyone could join; then give each link to that table only.
  3. Set 40 seconds, two rounds, two words per drawer.
  4. Paste the unit's word list.
  5. After the game, use the gallery for five minutes of talk.
