Halloween pictionary words for adults: 40 harder ideas that reward a quick mind

Ahmed Moaz · 27 October 2026

A pumpkin and a bat are fine for a seven-year-old. Adults tire of them by the second round. Halloween pictionary words for grown-ups work better when the picture is not the word: a pun, a feeling, a scene, a moment just before something goes wrong. Here are 40 I wrote for this page, in four groups. Pictionary is a brand owned by someone else; read it here as a name for the paper game, not for mine, and note that Boardit is unconnected to its owner.

Why are some Halloween words so much harder to draw?

A word is hard when it has no single object to point at. "Ghost writer" can be drawn two ways, and both are wrong until someone sees the joke. "Suspense" has no outline at all, so the drawer has to build a scene and trust the table.

My rule of thumb is simple. If you can name one thing to draw first, the word is easy. If you cannot, it is hard, and a hard word is where an adult table starts to shout.

Can you play a hard list online with friends?

Yes, and a long list suits it, because nobody has to find paper. Boardit's Draw and Guess is a drawing game online that I build, where the host opens a table and the others join with a name and nothing more.

A host may enter a custom list that the players never see, so this one can be pasted in as it stands. Each drawing defaults to 100 seconds, with five words offered to the drawer and three letter hints. Speed pays: a right guess is worth 100 plus 4 points per second left, so 70 seconds left scores 380, 40 left scores 260, and 10 left scores 140.

That scale matters on a hard list. A pun that takes the table half the clock still earns something, and the player who gets it at the last moment is not punished for the joke being clever.

How do you stop a hard list from stalling the game?

Three habits help. Allow one pass per player per round, so a drawer never sits frozen on "déjà vu". Put the abstract words in the middle of the list, not the start, so people warm up on moments before they meet feelings. And decide in advance that a loose guess still counts when it is funnier than the answer.

If a word keeps failing, drop it and move on. A list of forty is a menu, not a test, and a table that has laughed at three failures will happily try a fourth.
