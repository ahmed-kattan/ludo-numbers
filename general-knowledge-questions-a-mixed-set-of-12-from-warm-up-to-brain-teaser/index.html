General knowledge questions: a mixed set of 12, from warm-up to brain-teaser

Ahmed Moaz · 29 October 2026

A good set of general knowledge questions wanders. One question is about an ocean, the next about a bird, the next about a bone. Below are twelve in three bands of four: a warm-up, a middle band and a harder finish. Every answer has a short note, so this trivia quiz leaves people knowing a little more than they did. They are trivia questions for a table where nobody shares exactly the same interests.

Warm-up: four easy questions

  1. Which month gets an extra day in a leap year?
  2. Which is the largest ocean on Earth?
  3. Which instrument has 88 keys?
  4. What is the hardest natural substance?

The middle band: four medium questions

  1. Which bird can fly backwards?
  2. In which Indian city is the Taj Mahal?
  3. What is the biggest coral reef system in the world?
  4. What do we call a word that reads the same backwards, like "racecar"?

The finish: four harder questions

  1. Which is the smallest country in the world by area?
  2. What is the longest bone in the human body?
  3. Which Dutch painter cut off part of his own ear?
  4. What is the name for the study of earthquakes?

Answers and notes

  1. February. The extra day keeps the calendar in step with Earth's orbit, which takes about 365.24 days.
  2. The Pacific. It is bigger than all the land on Earth put together.
  3. The piano, which has 52 white keys and 36 black ones.
  4. Diamond. It is pure carbon, the same element as the graphite in pencils.
  5. The hummingbird, whose wings can beat around 50 times a second.
  6. Agra. Emperor Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal as a tomb for his wife, Mumtaz Mahal.
  7. The Great Barrier Reef, off Australia. It is the largest structure built by living things.
  8. A palindrome. "Was it a car or a cat I saw?" is one too, if you ignore the spaces.
  9. Vatican City, which covers about 0.44 square kilometres.
  10. The femur, or thigh bone, which is about a quarter of a person's height.
  11. Vincent van Gogh, who sold very few paintings in his lifetime. They now sell for tens of millions.
  12. Seismology. The word comes from the Greek seismos, meaning shaking.

Why mix the subjects?

A mixed set protects every guest. Among any group of friends, nobody knows everything, and nobody knows nothing. A person who freezes on the coral reef may be the one who knows the painter. If a set stays on one subject for twelve questions, you lose half the table by question four.

I also like to alternate the type of answer: a place, a thing, a word, a number. Questions 2, 6 and 9 are places. Questions 3, 4 and 10 are things you can touch. Question 8 is a word, and question 12 is a term. A spread like that keeps ears and minds from settling into one groove.

How should you score and time it?

Allow about fifteen seconds a question in the warm-up and up to thirty in the finish. One point each is enough for twelve questions, with no bonus for speed unless you play online, where the quickest right answer counts for more.

For a tie, hold back one number question that you have not read out, and let the nearest guess win.

Where could you run it with friends?

Anywhere with a table or a screen. When the table is spread over several cities, Boardit's Trivia Tavern shows everyone the same four-option question at the same moment. After you have run the set above by hand, it is an easy step towards online trivia with friends where nobody has to read aloud. If you are planning a whole trivia night, put twelve questions like these first, as the warm-up round.
