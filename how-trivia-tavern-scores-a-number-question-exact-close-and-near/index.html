How Trivia Tavern scores a number question: exact, close and near

Ahmed Moaz · 9 November 2026

Every round in Trivia Tavern ends with a question whose answer is a number. The scoring is not all or nothing, and it rewards a good guess.

The bands

Each number question comes with a distance that counts as close. The score depends on how far your guess is from the answer.

Your guessShare of the points
Exactly right100%
Within the close distance60%
Within twice that distance40%
Within four times that distance20%

The bonus

At a table of two or more, the closest guess also gets half the points again. So the nearest guess is rewarded, even if nobody is exact.

What counts as close

It depends on the question. For a year, the band is a few years. For a small count, the band is one. A question about when the Berlin Wall fell has a close distance of two years, so a guess of 1990 is within close, and 1987 is within twice.

What this means

  1. A guess is never worth nothing unless it is wildly out. Four times the distance still earns a fifth of the points.
  2. Exactness pays but is not required. A confident guess to within two years of a famous event scores 60%.
  3. Be the closest. The half-again bonus makes the nearest guess worth more than the others.

How to play it

Think of the event, then of its neighbours. If you remember the decade, give the middle of the decade. The shape of the bands rewards that.

Where to try it

Boardit's Trivia Tavern puts a number question at the end of every round when two or more people play. It is free, for two to eight players.

A worked example

Take the question about the year the Berlin Wall fell, where the answer is 1989 and the close distance is two years. A guess of 1989 earns full points. A guess between 1987 and 1991 earns 60%. Between 1985 and 1993, 40%. Between 1981 and 1997, 20%. The closest guess at the table gets half as much again on top.

What kind of numbers it asks for

The closest-number questions are spread across difficulty. In the set there are 45 easy, 108 medium and 125 hard ones, which is why a number question near the end of a round often feels harder than the questions before it.

The scoring in one line

Exact is full marks, close is most, and the closest of all gets a bonus.
