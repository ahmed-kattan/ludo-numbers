Guessing a year when you have no idea: three tricks for a number question

Ahmed Moaz · 12 November 2026

Every round of Trivia Tavern ends with a question whose answer is a number, often a year. If you do not know it, you can still score. The scoring has bands, and the nearest guess earns a bonus. These are three tricks.

Trick one: anchor to something you know

Find an event you can date, and place the question near it. If the question is about a famous building, ask whether it came before or after a war you can date. If it is about a film, ask whether people had colour television. One anchor can narrow a range from a century to a decade.

Trick two: guess the middle of your range

If you think the answer is between 1900 and 1930, say 1915. The scoring pays 60% within the close distance, 40% within twice that and 20% within four times. A guess in the middle of your range is the one most likely to land inside a band.

Trick three: do not stay blank

Even a poor guess can earn something. If the close distance is three years, a guess within 12 years still earns 20% of the points. A guess nowhere near earns nothing, but that is a rare outcome if you think for a few seconds.

A worked example

Suppose a question asks when a famous ship sank. You are unsure, but you know it was before aeroplanes were common, so you settle on 1910. If the answer is 1912 and the close distance is three years, you are inside the band and score 60%.

The bonus

At a table of two or more, the closest guess earns half as much again. If everyone is unsure, the one who has anchored well often wins it.

Where to practise

Trivia Tavern puts a number question at the end of every round when two or more people play.

A 200-point question

Bands scale with the points, so work out the stakes before you guess. On a 200-point question the bands pay 200, 120, 80 and 40. A guess you are only half sure of is still worth 40 if it is within four times the close distance.

The three tricks

Anchor, aim for the middle and never leave it blank.
