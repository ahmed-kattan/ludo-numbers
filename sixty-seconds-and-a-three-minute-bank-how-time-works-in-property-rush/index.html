Sixty seconds and a three-minute bank: how time works in Property Rush

Ahmed Moaz · 10 November 2026

Property Rush is a long game, and a long game needs a clock that is fair to slow thinkers. Each turn has 60 seconds. After that, you can use your own bank of three minutes of extra time, for the whole game.

How it works

You have 60 seconds on each turn. If you need longer, the clock moves into your bank. Three minutes is the total, not a fresh three minutes each turn. Once the bank is empty, you are back to what is left of the 60 seconds.

When the time is gone, a bot finishes that turn safely. Miss two turns in a row and a bot plays your seat until you return. Nobody is removed for being away.

A little arithmetic

Suppose a game has 20 turns for you. If you use the whole 60 seconds every time, you have used 20 minutes. If you use 75 seconds on five of them, you have used 75 seconds minus 60, so 15 seconds from the bank five times, or 75 seconds in total. The bank only matters on the hard decisions.

The bank is a budget. A player who spends it early has nothing left for the end game, when the choices are hardest.

Advice

  1. Do not spend the bank on easy turns. Buying a city you can afford takes seconds.
  2. Save it for trades and mortgages. Those need the most thinking.
  3. Check it before an auction. Bidding takes time, too.

What the host can do

The host cannot change these numbers, but can choose settings that make the game quicker, such as a smaller starting purse. Fast dice are also on unless the host turns them off.

What a bot does with your seat

A bot is only a safety net. It finishes a turn safely and holds the seat. It is not meant to play your strategy, so if you know you will be away for a while, say so and let the table decide whether to pause.

How long a lap takes

A lap of 40 tiles takes about 5.7 turns at an average roll of seven, ignoring cards and Prison. At a full 60 seconds a turn, a lap is under six minutes of one player's clock, which is why the three-minute bank is spent slowly if you play quickly.

The bank in one line

Sixty seconds a turn, three minutes in reserve, and a bot as a safety net.
