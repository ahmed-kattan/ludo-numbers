Houses first: why three houses on every city pay back in about 26 to 30 stops

Ahmed Moaz · 13 November 2026

A bare city pays back slowly. A city with houses on it pays back fast. I worked out how much faster, using the stop rates from my simulation of the Property Rush board.

The method

For each city I took its price and its rent, then multiplied by how often an opponent's token stops there. That gives the income per opponent stop. Dividing the price, plus the houses, by that income gives the number of opponent stops needed to pay everything back. I ran the simulation on 17 October 2026, with 3,000,000 turns and the default dice.

The results

Torontoabout 293 stopsabout 26 stops
Osakaabout 368 stopsabout 28 stops
Parisabout 446 stopsabout 30 stops

The drop is by a factor of ten or more. That is what a house does. Rent rises steeply with each house, while a house costs a small part of the price.

Why the doubling matters too

Owning every city in a country doubles the rent before any house is built. That alone halves the bare-land figures. Houses then do the rest.

What it means for play

  1. Do not sit on a complete set. A set without houses is a slow investment.
  2. Build evenly. The rules require it, and the bank has 32 houses to share.
  3. Spend on houses before a new city. A third city with no houses pays back far slower than houses on the two you have.
  4. Choose countries that rivals land on. The payback figures use the stop rate, so a busy city repays faster.

The caution

These are averages for a single token, not a promise. A real game depends on where tokens actually go, and on how fast a rival's cash runs out.

Putting it in order

If I had 400 to spend in the middle of a game, I would put it into houses on a country I already own before I bought a new city. The houses lift the payback from hundreds of stops to dozens. A new bare city, by comparison, starts the slow climb again. The only reason to buy first is to stop a rival completing a set.

What a build costs in each country

Houses first is only possible where you can afford them. Building to a hotel costs 250 per city in the UAE and Egypt, 500 in Italy and France, 750 in Japan and Germany and 1,000 in the Netherlands and Canada, so the cheap countries are where this plan is quickest.

The lesson

The money is in the houses.
