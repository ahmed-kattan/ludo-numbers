Imposter game words for a work team: 30 safe words for an away day or a call

Ahmed Moaz · 23 October 2026

A team game such as Who's the Spy needs words that are safe for everyone. Nobody wants a word that points at a colleague, touches a sore subject or turns the room awkward. These imposter game words for a work team are neutral, shared and a little funny, chosen for an away day, a team meeting or a spy game online with friends from the office.

What makes a word safe at work?

It is something everyone has met, it does not name a person or a client, and it does not touch on anything sensitive such as pay, performance or politics. A good test: would you be happy for your manager to hear it? If so, it is safe.

How do you run it at work?

Open with a short explanation: everyone but one gets the same word, the odd one out is told only the category. Play three rounds, no more. Stop while people want more. At work, people appreciate a game that ends on time.

What categories fit these words?

The three lists above can serve as categories: the office, meetings and calls, work habits. Tell the spy the category aloud. If you play online with the game's own words, its 25 categories (places, food, jobs and professions, sports, household objects, school and office, and more) cover plenty of common ground, and the host can type in the table's own words.

How do you keep it inclusive?

Let everyone speak. Do not make anyone the permanent spy. Avoid words that rely on knowledge of a particular team or client. And let new joiners play, because the game is a good way to learn each other's voice.

How do you handle senior people in the room?

Treat them as players. A manager who is the spy and fumbles a clue does the team a favour: it shows that nobody is above the game. If a senior person tends to dominate, seat them in the middle of the order and ask the quieter players for their view before the vote. The game works well when everyone is equally unsure.

What if the team is remote?

Play by link on a call, with each person on their own device. Keep the video on if the connection allows, since faces are half the evidence. If someone is in a noisy place, they can join with audio only. A remote team usually needs the game more than an office team, because it has fewer chances to laugh together.

What do you gain?

A team that has laughed together gives clearer feedback afterwards. A game of ten minutes costs nothing, and it shows who thinks quickly, who listens and who bluffs well. These are not performance data; they are the ordinary sparks of a team that likes each other.

Where can you play?

At Boardit, a free imposter game online with friends, a table seats three to eight with a link and no account. For a bigger away day, use one phone for up to twelve, or run two tables.

Two words per clue

Keep the same rule for every round: no clue longer than a couple of words. It keeps the game fast and keeps anyone from making a speech.
