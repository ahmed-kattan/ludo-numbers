Space trivia questions on astronauts and missions, from the first spacewalk to a Mars rover

Ahmed Moaz · 2 November 2026

People like space history because the stories are short and the heroes have names. These eight space trivia questions follow the missions roughly in order, from a spacewalk in 1965 to a rover that landed on Mars in 2021. Each one has an answer, a small detail to read out, and a reference page in the front matter. Together they make a compact trivia quiz that takes about ten minutes.

Eight questions in date order

  1. Who made the first spacewalk, in March 1965?
  2. What do Russian space travellers get called?
  3. Which US space agency was founded in 1958?
  4. Which Apollo mission was the first crewed flight to orbit the Moon, in December 1968?
  5. Which mission put Neil Armstrong on the Moon, and who followed him out?
  6. Who was the first American woman in space?
  7. Who was the first African American woman in space, in 1992?
  8. Which NASA rover landed on Mars in 2021 and carried a small helicopter?

The answers

  1. Alexei Leonov, during the Voskhod 2 mission on 18 March 1965. He was outside for 12 minutes and 9 seconds.
  2. Cosmonauts. The word "astronaut" is used for American and many other crews.
  3. NASA.
  4. Apollo 8. Frank Borman, Jim Lovell and William Anders made ten orbits without landing, and Anders took the famous Earthrise photograph.
  5. Apollo 11. Buzz Aldrin followed him. The landing was on 20 July 1969, US time, and the moonwalk lasted about two and a half hours.
  6. Sally Ride, who flew on the shuttle Challenger in June 1983. She was 32, and the youngest American to fly in space at that time.
  7. Mae Jemison, who flew on the shuttle Endeavour in September 1992.
  8. Perseverance. The helicopter was named Ingenuity.

Why does the Apollo 8 question matter?

Because it comes before the landing and is easy to overlook. Apollo 8 never touched down. It was the first crewed craft to leave Earth's gravity and circle the Moon, and the crew saw the Earth rise over the lunar horizon. A table that knows only the 1969 story gets a pleasant surprise here.

Question 5 has a trap built in. People answer "Neil Armstrong" to the first half and forget there is a second half. If a player names both, give a point for each name.

How should you order the round?

Start with 2 and 3, which are short recall. Move on to 1, 6 and 8. Questions 4, 5 and 7 are the ones that separate people who read about space from people who only like it. When two players tie, ask how many orbits the Apollo 8 crew made around the Moon. The nearest guess wins. The answer is ten, and a guess is allowed to be wild.

One thing I left out

I did not include any question about the number of people who have walked on the Moon, or about records for time spent in orbit. Those figures change when new crews fly, and a quiz answer should still be true next year. I re-read the three bank entries and fetched the other pages on 27 October 2026.
