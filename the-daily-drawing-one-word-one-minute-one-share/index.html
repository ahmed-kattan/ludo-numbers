The daily drawing: one word, one minute, one share

Ahmed Moaz · 12 November 2026

Some people want a game every day and not an evening. Draw and Guess has a small daily version: one word to draw in a minute and share.

What it is

Each day there is one word. You get a minute to draw it. When you are done, you share the drawing.

Why a minute

A minute is short enough to do between jobs and long enough to draw a shape. It also means nobody can get precious. A one-minute drawing is allowed to be silly.

What it is not

It is not a table game. A full game of Draw and Guess has two to twelve players taking turns, while the daily drawing is a single word for you to draw and share when you like.

Ways to use it

  1. A morning habit. Draw the word before you read the news.
  2. A group-chat game. Post your drawing and see what the others made of the same word.
  3. A warm-up. Do it before you play a table game, to get your hand moving.
  4. A way to learn. A minute of drawing a day adds up over a month.

What makes a good minute

Start with the shape that identifies the word. Do not shade. If you finish early, add a clue that gives the word context.

Draw to a friend

Related, but different: Draw to a friend lets you draw a word and send a link. Your friend watches the drawing come to life and has three tries to guess it, then draws one back.

What a one-minute drawing teaches

The first thing it teaches is what to leave out. With a minute, you cannot draw every leaf, so you learn which two or three shapes carry the word. The second is to commit: a line, once drawn, stays. The third is to share without apologising. A drawing that took a minute does not need an excuse, and the people who see it are usually kind.

Where to find it

You can find both on Boardit, which runs in a browser and needs no download.

The habit in a sentence

A minute a day with one word.
