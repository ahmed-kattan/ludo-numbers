Making a quiz fair for mixed ages: eight small choices

Ahmed Moaz · 8 November 2026

A quiz with a parent, a teenager and a grandparent is a good test of a host. These are the choices I would make.

Questions

  1. Keep them about well-known things. Trivia Tavern's questions are about famous things and are not too hard, so a wide age range can try them.
  2. Use mixed categories. Mixed means everyone gets something, whatever they know.

Rules

  1. Keep the closest-number question. Every round ends with a question whose answer is a number, such as a year. The closest answer wins, so a lucky guess counts.
  2. Switch the final bet on or off. It lets a player put part of their score on one last question. Leave it off for children, on for confident players.
  3. Choose the slower mode. The host can pick a scoring mode where a quick answer earns only a few more points, which gives slower players a fair turn.

The table

  1. Make teams. A child and a grandparent on the same team shares the load.
  2. Keep it short. The median game in Boardit's table log was ten minutes between 13 September and 30 October 2026. Two games make a good evening.
  3. Say the score out loud. Reading out the board keeps everyone included.

Why fairness matters

A quiz that one person always wins is a quiz people stop playing. The aim is a table that wants a second game.

One thing I would not do

I would not pick a hard difficulty for a mixed group. A question that nobody knows teaches nothing, and a question that everybody knows is a gift to the person who spoke first.

What to do about a dispute

A dispute is a good sign, because it means people care. Settle it by the answer shown on screen. If someone still disagrees, agree to look it up after the game and carry on. A quiz that stops for ten minutes to check one fact loses the room.

Choosing the time

Aim for two games of about ten minutes with a short break. Children tire first, and a quiz that ends while they still want one more is a quiz they will ask for again.

Choosing categories for a mixed table

The bank holds more than 2,500 questions, and the categories are not the same size: Entertainment has the most at 491 and Food & drink the fewest at 142. For a table of mixed ages, choosing Food & drink and General knowledge is not a smaller game, but it is a narrower pool, so questions can repeat sooner across games.

Before you begin

Decide who reads the answers aloud. It is usually the host, and it helps if the host is calm about disputes.
