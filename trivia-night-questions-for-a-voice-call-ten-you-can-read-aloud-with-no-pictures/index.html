Trivia night questions for a voice call: ten you can read aloud with no pictures

Ahmed Moaz · 31 October 2026

Plenty of trivia night questions need something to look at: a logo, a flag, a map. On a voice call, or on a video call where half the table is looking at the cooker, that breaks down. These ten trivia questions are written to be heard. Each one can be said once, understood, and answered without a screen. Below them are the rules I follow when I write a question for the ear.

The ten questions

  1. Which Roman god of war gave his name to a red planet?
  2. What is the Hindu greeting, made with pressed palms, that roughly means "I bow to you"?
  3. Someone who collects postage stamps has a name for their hobby. What is the collector called?
  4. Which Mexican spirit is made from the blue agave plant?
  5. Which author wrote the novel that gave us "Big Brother" and "Room 101"?
  6. Which Spanish painter made the huge 1937 anti-war picture named after a bombed Basque town?
  7. What is the reading system for blind people, built from raised dots, named after its young inventor?
  8. Which Norse explorer may have reached North America about 500 years before Columbus?
  9. Which blood type is known as the universal donor?
  10. Closest number: how many weeks does a typical human pregnancy last?

The answers

  1. Mars. The month of March is named after him too.
  2. Namaste.
  3. A philatelist. The Penny Black, issued in Britain in 1840, was the first postage stamp.
  4. Tequila.
  5. George Orwell, for Nineteen Eighty-Four.
  6. Pablo Picasso, who painted Guernica.
  7. Braille. Louis Braille invented it in 1824, when he was 15.
  8. Leif Erikson. A Viking settlement has been found at L'Anse aux Meadows in Canada.
  9. O negative.
  10. About 40 weeks.

What makes a question work with no screen?

Put the question word first and the clue last. "Which author wrote the novel that gave us Big Brother" tells the listener what kind of answer to expect before the details arrive, so they can listen with a purpose.

Avoid answers that sound alike. If the answer has two spellings or two common pronunciations, someone will argue. A name that is easy to say but hard to spell is fine on a call, because nobody has to type it. A name that sounds like another word is not.

Skip anything that asks the table to look at something. "Which of these flags" and "what is shown here" are out. So are questions that depend on a list of options too long to hold in your head. Four choices is the limit, and I read them with letters.

How do you handle the number question on a call?

Spoken guesses spoil a closest-number question, because the last person to speak can pick a number just beside the first. Ask everyone to send theirs to the host in a private message, then read them all out. If a chat is not possible, use a count-in: everyone says a number at the same moment, and the host writes down what they heard from each person.

Can I play online trivia with friends instead?

If reading aloud is more work than you want, Boardit's Trivia Tavern puts each question on every player's own screen, with four answers and one right. The host can set 10, 15, 20 or 30 seconds a question, and up to eight can play. You stay on the call to talk, while the trivia quiz keeps score.

I would still read the questions above aloud for a smaller group. Online trivia with friends works better when the host can add a line of context after the answer, and the call is where that happens.

Setting a pace for a heard-only trivia night

Read the question twice, wait about 15 seconds, then give the answer and one extra fact. Keep the extra fact to a sentence. On a call people drift if the host fills silence with a story, and they miss the next question.

Ten questions with that rhythm run for ten to fifteen minutes. After a break, a second round works well.
