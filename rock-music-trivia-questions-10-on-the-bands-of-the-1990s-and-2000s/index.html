Rock music trivia questions: 10 on the bands of the 1990s and 2000s

Ahmed Moaz · 21 October 2026

For many players, rock means the bands of their teens: the grunge, the britpop and the stadium choruses of the 1990s and 2000s. These ten rock music trivia questions cover those years, each answered with a note. Use them at a party or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. Which Seattle band, led by Kurt Cobain, recorded Smells Like Teen Spirit?
  2. Which band sang Livin' on a Prayer?
  3. Which band sang In the End and Numb?
  4. Which band sang Seven Nation Army, a famous football-stadium chant?
  5. Which band had a hit with Mr. Brightside?
  6. Which band was formed by Nirvana's drummer, Dave Grohl?
  7. Which British band from Sheffield sang Do I Wanna Know?
  8. Which British band sang Creep and Karma Police?
  9. Which band sang Song 2, with its famous woo-hoo?
  10. Which British band released the 1995 album (What's the Story) Morning Glory?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Nirvana. Their album Nevermind knocked Michael Jackson's Dangerous off the top of the charts.
  2. Bon Jovi, named after the singer Jon Bon Jovi, whose real surname is Bongiovi.
  3. Linkin Park. Their album Hybrid Theory is one of the top-selling debuts of the century.
  4. The White Stripes. Fans have sung the riff in stadiums all over the world.
  5. The Killers. Mr. Brightside has spent over 400 weeks in the UK top 100.
  6. Foo Fighters. Dave Grohl played almost every instrument on the first album.
  7. Arctic Monkeys. Their first album was the fastest-selling debut in UK history at the time.
  8. Radiohead, who released In Rainbows on a pay-what-you-want basis in 2007.
  9. Blur. Song 2 was meant as a joke on American grunge.
  10. Oasis. The album is one of the top-selling in UK history.

How hard is the set?

Questions 1 to 4 are comfortable for anyone who listened to the radio. Questions 5 to 8 are medium. Questions 9 and 10 are the nostalgic kind that split a table by age. A good tie-break is to ask the year of Nevermind, with a point for the nearest guess.

How do you use it on the night?

As a trivia night for a birthday, run this set after the cake, when the room is relaxed. A little singing is part of the deal.

What makes this era so quizzable?

Everyone has a memory attached. A chorus shouted in a school hall, a riff chanted at a match, a song played at every wedding. A quizmaster who plays a few seconds of each hit before asking the question turns the round into a reunion. The facts are almost secondary to the feeling, and that is exactly why the round works.

How do you handle different ages?

Pair a younger player with an older one, and give a bonus point for the team that spans the widest gap. The older player knows the bands; the younger knows the streaming figures. A mixed team has the strongest chance, and the conversation between the generations is better than the score.

What do the notes teach?

Mostly the odd little facts behind famous songs: a joke that became a hit, a riff that became a chant, a drummer who played everything. Those details are what people repeat to each other. If a team gets all ten, ask for the name of the first Oasis album, a bonus question from the same bank.

The sound of a decade

Ask the table to choose the one song that sums up the era, and defend it in a sentence. The results are better than any trivia.
