Movie trivia questions on famous quotes: nine lines people get slightly wrong

Ahmed Moaz · 30 October 2026

Almost everyone quotes films from memory, and memory improves them. These movie trivia questions ask about nine famous lines where the popular version is a little off. I use only a few words of each line, and every correction links to a source page.

The nine questions

  1. People say "Play it again, Sam" about Casablanca. Which word did Ilsa actually use instead of "again"?
  2. People say "Luke, I am your father." In The Empire Strikes Back, does Darth Vader say Luke's name in that line?
  3. Does Captain Kirk say "Beam me up, Scotty" in the original Star Trek series?
  4. In Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, what does the Queen call the mirror on the wall?
  5. In the film Apollo 13, the line is "Houston, we have a problem." What tense did the real crew use?
  6. People say "If you build it, they will come." Which pronoun is in the film's line?
  7. Where does Dorothy put Toto's name in her line about Kansas?
  8. Han Solo says the Millennium Falcon made the Kessel Run in less than twelve of what unit?
  9. People say "Do you feel lucky, punk?" Which short word does Harry Callahan use instead of "you"?

The answers

  1. "Once." Ilsa asks Sam to play it once for old times' sake.
  2. No. The line is "No. I am your father." The name Luke is not in it.
  3. No. The line was never said in the original series. The nearest is "Scotty, beam me up" in a later film.
  4. "Magic mirror." The popular "Mirror, mirror" is a later habit.
  5. The real crew said "we've had a problem", in the past tense.
  6. "He." The line is "If you build it, he will come."
  7. At the start. Dorothy says "Toto, I've a feeling we're not in Kansas any more", which is not how most of us say it.
  8. Parsecs, which measure distance, not time. Fans have explained the line in several ways, and a later film made a joke of it.
  9. "Ya." The line is "Do ya feel lucky, punk?"

Why do we all misquote the same way?

My guess is that a line which works on screen does not always work when it is spoken in a pub. We drop the name, move the pronoun and add a word to make it sound complete. That is not a reason to be superior about it. A quiz that spots the difference is making a point about memory, not about intelligence.

What kinds of slip are these?

Sorting the nine into types makes a good talking point after the answers.

  • A word is added or dropped: "again" in Casablanca, "Luke" in The Empire Strikes Back.
  • A word is swapped for a more natural one: "he" for "they" in the baseball field film, "ya" for "you" in the police film.
  • A tense is smoothed out: "we've had" became "we have" in the Apollo 13 line.
  • A line is invented by repetition: "Beam me up, Scotty" was never said in the series that made it famous.
  • A word is moved: Toto's name at the start, not the end.
  • A technical term is misread: parsecs are a distance, and the boast still became a famous line.

Which type do you make most often? In most of the nine, the changed version is shorter or easier to say, which is probably why it survives.

How do you score a question like these?

Give a full point for the corrected word, not for the whole line. A half-point is fair for someone who knows the popular version is wrong but cannot say what the right one is. That keeps the round about spotting the slip.

Writing your own misquote round

Pick a favourite film, find its script or a trusted source page, and set the real line against the one you remember. Keep the quote to a few words, and write down the source. Three or four of these make a good short round for a trivia night.
