A game night for two couples and one sofa: low-effort games for a small flat

Ahmed Moaz · 17 October 2026

Four adults, one sofa, no dining table, and a coffee table covered in snacks. A small flat with two couples in it cannot lean on a table. It can count on everyone having a phone, so plan around phones and talk.

Why the talk matters more than the game

There is research on this set-up. In a 2010 study, the psychologist Richard Slatcher paired 60 dating couples into groups of four. Half the groups worked through questions that invited real disclosure; the other half made small talk. The disclosure groups felt closer to the other couple and to their own partner. A third of those couples got back in touch with the other pair afterwards, against none in the small-talk groups.

A game is not a disclosure task, but it pushes the same way: it gives four people a reason to argue, bluff and explain themselves. So pick games to play with another couple that make people talk, and keep screen-staring short.

Sit partners apart

The easiest of all game night ideas for couples costs nothing: don't let partners sit side by side. Couples read each other's faces too well, and in a bluffing game that is an unfair edge over the other pair. Alternate the seating, one from each couple in turn, and every glance across the sofa is a fair one.

It also changes the talk: at a couples game night, sitting next to someone you know less well is closer to what that study set up. This is a house rule, not a finding.

One phone, passed along the sofa

Pass the phone games, like most online party games, suit a sofa because nobody needs to reach a table. My own example is Who's the Spy. One phone goes along the sofa; each person privately sees either the shared secret word or a card saying they are the spy, then hands it on. The clues are spoken, and the table votes on who was bluffing.

On Boardit's version the phone fetches one card at a time, only for the person named on screen. Whoever holds it cannot flick ahead and see a partner's role. This is the round where the talking happens, so play it first.

Ludo for 4 players, one board, four phones

For a second game, everyone takes out their own phone. Ludo fits two couples exactly: the classic board has four colours, one each, so nobody is left watching from the arm of the sofa.

Each person plays from their own screen, so the board does not need the coffee table. Ludo is the long one, though, so start it only if the evening has time left.

A small apartment game night, in order

  1. Drinks and snacks first, games second.
  2. The one-phone bluffing round, with partners seated apart.
  3. Ludo on four phones if the night is young; skip it if not.
  4. Finish with talk, not a game; the study above found the closeness in the talking.

As double date games at home go, that order needs no shopping, no table and no box, and it suits any games for 4 adults who share a sofa. That is a double date planned in five minutes.

What a phone-based evening for two couples leaves out

Charge the phones first: Ludo needs one each, the bluffing round only one in total. I have not hosted this exact evening myself, so treat the order as a starting point.

The research has limits too. Slatcher's couples were paired with another couple in a lab and given set questions, not friends on a sofa playing games. The study found that disclosure between couples builds closeness; whether any particular game does is untested. Of all the ideas for adults here, the cheapest needs no phone: ask the other couple a question you actually want answered.
