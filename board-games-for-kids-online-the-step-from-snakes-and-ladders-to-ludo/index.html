Board games for kids online: the step from Snakes and Ladders to Ludo

Ahmed Moaz · 5 November 2026

After a child has played a few rounds of Snakes and Ladders, the next question from a parent is usually which game comes next. Among board games for kids online, Ludo is a natural second step, because it keeps the die and the race and adds the first real decision. I build both games, so I can say which parts carry over and which parts are new. Nothing here is a study of how children learn. It is my reading of the two rule sets.

What does Snakes and Ladders teach that Ludo needs?

Four habits carry over.

  • Throwing a die and moving that many squares.
  • Reading a number and counting on.
  • Waiting for a turn on a 30-second clock.
  • Needing an exact number to finish. In Snakes and Ladders you must land on 100 exactly, and in Ludo the piece must reach home exactly.

A child who has met the exact-number rule once will not be surprised by it the second time.

What is new in Ludo?

Choice. Each player has four pieces, so a throw leaves a question: which one moves? There are also several new rules.

  • A piece leaves base only on a six.
  • Another throw is earned by a six, by a capture, or by bringing a piece home, though a run of three sixes cancels the turn.
  • Landing on a lone opponent sends it back to base.
  • Eight squares on the track are safe, and a pair of the same colour on one square is protected.
  • The table is smaller: two to four players, against two to eight in Snakes and Ladders.

The six alone is a good lesson in probability. In my calculation, a player needs six throws on average to see the first six, and in three throws the chance of none is about 58%, which is (5/6) multiplied by itself three times. Waiting for a six can feel unfair to a child, and it helps to know it is only arithmetic.

Can you make Ludo gentler?

Yes, the host has options. Quick Ludo gives each player two pieces instead of four, a game of about five minutes. Captures can be switched off, so pieces share squares and nobody is sent back. Extra throws can be turned off, and a setting that allows several tries at a six helps when waiting in base is the worst part.

For a first Ludo game, I would pick Quick Ludo with captures off. It keeps the choice, which piece to move, and removes the sting.

How do you move from one game to the next?

Gradually.

  1. Keep playing Snakes and Ladders until the child asks for a choice, or a game feels too easy.
  2. Introduce Ludo on a quiet day with two players and Quick Ludo.
  3. Point out the old rules first: the die, the counting, the exact finish.
  4. Add captures once the child is comfortable moving a piece.
  5. Go back to Snakes and Ladders whenever a rest is wanted.

In both games the host can fill an empty seat with a bot, and a bot also takes over a seat after two lapses back to back. That is a quiet help when a grandparent or sibling cannot make it.

Where do you start?

The starting point is the easier game. Boardit seats up to eight in a browser, free to join, with friends or family on separate devices. A snakes and ladders game shows a child how a turn feels before any choice is asked of them, and snakes and ladders online is a short step from there to the next game.

What if a child skips ahead?

Some children will not wait for the order. They watch an older sibling play Ludo and want it at once. Let them try. The Quick option keeps the cost low, and Snakes and Ladders will still be there on the days when choosing feels like too much.
