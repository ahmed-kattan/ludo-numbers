European capital cities trivia questions: 10 with the story of each city

Ahmed Moaz · 22 October 2026

Capital cities are a classic of the quiz, and the strongest ones come with a story. These ten capital cities trivia questions cover Europe from Lisbon to Oslo, each answered with a note. Use them at a table, in a classroom or as a round in a trivia quiz online with friends.

The questions

  1. What is the capital of Italy?
  2. What is the capital of Spain?
  3. What is the capital of Germany?
  4. What is the capital of Greece?
  5. What is the capital of Ireland?
  6. What is the capital of Portugal?
  7. What is the capital of Austria?
  8. What is the capital of Sweden?
  9. What is the capital of Norway?
  10. What is the capital of Denmark?

The answers, with a note on each

  1. Rome. It has a whole country inside it: Vatican City.
  2. Madrid. It is one of the highest capitals in Europe, about 650 metres above sea level.
  3. Berlin. It was split in two by the Berlin Wall from 1961 to 1989.
  4. Athens. It is one of the world's oldest cities, lived in for over 3,000 years.
  5. Dublin. Guinness has been brewed there since 1759.
  6. Lisbon. Much of it was rebuilt after a huge earthquake in 1755.
  7. Vienna. It is famous for its classical music, from Mozart to Strauss.
  8. Stockholm. It is built on 14 islands.
  9. Oslo. The Nobel Peace Prize is prizeed there every year.
  10. Copenhagen. The Little Mermaid statue there is only 1.25 metres tall.

How hard is the set?

These are the easy end of the category, and a well-travelled table should get most. They suit children and beginners. To make the set harder, ask for the country as well as the capital in reverse, or add the capitals of the Balkans from the hard end of the bank.

How do you use it on the night?

At a trivia night with a geography round, put the capitals just after the break, when the room is warm and competitive.

Why do capitals make good quiz questions?

Because everyone has half-learned them. A table will agree on Rome and argue about Bern. The set above is the confident half, and the notes add something to chew on: a country inside a city, a city on 14 islands, a statue smaller than a child. These small oddities are what people remember.

How do you use it with children?

Make it a map game. Print a blank map of Europe, read a question and let a child stick a pin on the answer. By the end, the map has ten pins and the children have learned where things are. A family with a map on the wall can repeat the game whenever there is a spare ten minutes.

What do the notes tell you about the cities?

Mostly that capitals have odd stories. Vienna's classical music, Lisbon's earthquake and Stockholm's islands are the reasons a capital sticks in memory. A quizmaster who reads the note slowly gets a nod from the people who have visited and a spark of interest from the people who have not. If a team gets everything right, ask for the country's second city as a bonus; the answers are rarely as confident.

A word on the order

Start with the capitals everyone knows, and finish with the tricky ones. A quiz that begins with success keeps people playing. The harder capitals in the bank, such as those of the Balkans, make a good second round.
